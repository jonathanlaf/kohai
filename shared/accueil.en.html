Kohai 後輩

My personal study notes on Shotokan karate katas.

In the dojo, the kohai is the student who learns from their elders, the senpai. These notes keep them company between classes: for each kata, its spirit, its embusen, every move illustrated and explained, a video, the common mistakes and the Japanese vocabulary. Each set of notes prints with its own table of contents. They complement the sensei’s teaching without replacing it. Who am I and why this site?

What is a kata 型?

A kata (型, “form”) is a set sequence of techniques, performed alone against imaginary opponents. It is the memory of karate: each kata passes techniques, stances, movements and fighting principles from one generation of practitioners to the next.

Each kata follows a precise pattern on the floor, the embusen, starts and ends at the same spot, and opens and closes with a bow. It is judged on the accuracy of the stances, the power of the techniques (kime), the intensity (kihaku) and the awareness that remains until the last move (zanshin).

Shotokan has 26 katas, grouped into four families. They are learned in order, as you move up through the grades.
