Belts and ranks (Obi) 帯

The colour of the belt (obi, 帯) shows the rank. There are two series: kyu 級, the student ranks in coloured belts, and dan 段, the black-belt ranks.

The stripes on the brown belt count down the remaining kyu; the bars on the black belt count the dan. The colours, and the kyu number of the coloured belts, change from one association to another (some add purple or half-belts): this site follows the most common order, but your dojo has the last word.

Kyu and dan

  • Kyu 級, “class”: counted down. The lower the number, the further along you are, up to 1st kyu, the last one before black belt.
  • Dan 段, “degree, step”: counted up, from 1st to 10th dan (see below). High dan ranks recognize years of practice and teaching (see titles).

Shodan and the dan ranks 初段

Shodan means “first level”, “first degree”. The word joins two kanji: 初 sho, beginning, first, and 段 dan, level, step.

In karate, shodan is the first black belt. It doesn’t mean you have mastered karate: it shows you have mastered the fundamentals and are ready to begin deeper study. The black belt is a start, not a finish.

The series goes on up to 10th dan, Jūdan 十段.

The same words number the katas of a series: Heian Shodan is the first Heian kata, Tekki Nidan the second Tekki kata. In a kata name, Shodan gives its place in the series, not the black belt: Heian Shodan is learned at white belt.
