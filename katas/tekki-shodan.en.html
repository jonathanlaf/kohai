Tekki Shodan 鉄騎初段

Iron horseman, first level

Tekki Shodan, the first of the three Tekki kata, has 29 moves, with two Kiai: on the 15th and the 29th move. It is required for the brown belt grading, 2nd kyu. Its embusen is a single straight line, travelled sideways.

Also read, common to every kata: The spirit of the kata and Common mistakes.

Breaking down the kata 型, step by step

The whole kata is done in Kiba Dachi (horse stance), except for the three crossing steps (Kosa Dachi), on a single line: you never move forward or backward. It is also the first kata that does not start in the usual Yoi position: the feet are together, the open left hand resting on the right hand. The head turns count as moves and must be sharp. It teaches the hooking block (kake uke), the hook punch (kagi zuki), the short punch (ura zuki) and the returning-wave kick (nami gaeshi), found only in the Tekki kata. The second half of the kata (16 to 29) repeats the first on the other side.

Embusen 演武線 (floor pattern)

The kata follows a single straight line. You set off to the right with a crossing step (1) and stop at the right end (2 to 6). You come back to the left with another crossing step (7) to the left end, where moves 8 to 20 are done (Kiai on the 15th). A last crossing step (21) takes you back to the right end (22 to 29, Kiai on the 29th). With Yame, you slowly bring the right foot in and return to the starting point.

Tekki Shodan 鉄騎初段 kata tutorial

How to read this tutorial

Each move takes one block: on the left, its number and, when there is one, the illustration; on the right, the technique (its Japanese name in bold, the translation below), the stance and the target level. The “Footwork” line explains how to move, and the “Note” line, when there is one, gives a detail to remember.

Jodan is the head (the face); Chudan, the trunk, from the shoulders to the belt (the solar plexus, just under the sternum); Gedan, below the belt (the lower belly). Each level aims at a precise point, not just a height: a chudan punch goes to the solar plexus, not the shoulder. For a block, the level shows the zone being protected.

The shape of the number is the same as on the embusen, to link the two: it shows the stance, and red marks a Kiai.
