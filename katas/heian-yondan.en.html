Heian Yondan 平安四段

Peaceful mind, fourth level

Heian Yondan, the fourth Heian kata, has 27 moves, with two Kiai: on the 13th and the 25th move. It is the longest of the Heian kata. It is required for the grading from green belt to blue belt. Its embusen is almost shaped like an “I.”

What this kata trains

Heian Yondan is the kata of leg techniques: two side kicks (yoko geri keage), three front kicks (mae geri) and a knee strike (hiza geri). It also introduces the back-of-hand block (haishu uke), the X-block (juji uke), the wedge block (kakiwake uke), the elbow strike into the palm and the crossed-feet stance (Kosa Dachi). The rhythm alternates slow moves (1, 2, 14 and 18) and fast ones. You must finish the kata exactly at the starting point.

Embusen 演武線 (line of movement)

The kata follows an almost “I”-shaped pattern, with a “V” at the far end. Moves 1 and 2 are at the starting point, to the left then to the right. You move forward on the centre line (3 and 4), do the kicks and elbow strikes to the left then to the right (5 to 10), then go forward to the end of the line (11 to 13, Kiai on the 13th). The two rear diagonals start from there, first to the left (14 to 17), then to the right (18 to 21). You then come back on the centre line toward the starting point (22 to 25, Kiai on the 25th). The turn of move 26 puts the right foot down just behind the starting point, and move 27 brings you back exactly to the starting point.

Heian Yondan 平安四段 kata tutorial

How to read this tutorial

Each move takes one block: on the left, its number and, when there is one, the illustration; on the right, the technique (its Japanese name in bold, the translation below), the stance and the target level. The “Footwork” line explains how to move, and the “Note” line, when there is one, gives a detail to remember.

Jodan is the head (the face); Chudan, the trunk, from the shoulders to the belt (the solar plexus, just under the sternum); Gedan, below the belt (the lower belly). Each level aims at a precise point, not just a height: a chudan punch goes to the solar plexus, not the shoulder. For a block, the level shows the zone being protected.

The shape of the number is the same as on the embusen, to link the two: it shows the stance, and red marks a Kiai.
