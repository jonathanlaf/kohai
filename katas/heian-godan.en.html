Heian Godan 平安五段

Peaceful mind, fifth level

Heian Godan, the fifth and last Heian kata, has 23 moves, with two Kiai: on the 12th and the 19th move. It is required for the grading from blue belt to brown belt. Its embusen is shaped like a “T.”

What this kata trains

Heian Godan is the kata of variety: it links many new techniques and stances, such as gyaku zuki in Kokutsu Dachi, upward and downward X-blocks (juji uke), the crescent kick (mikazuki geri), the L-shaped stance (Renoji Dachi), the manji uke double block and, above all, a jump that dodges an attack to the legs and lands in Kosa Dachi. The rhythm alternates strong, fast moments and slower, more focused ones (moves 3, 6, 14 and 22). It is often the hardest of the five Heian: several techniques need good stability and precise weight shifts.

Embusen 演武線 (line of movement)

The kata follows a “T”-shaped pattern. Moves 1 to 6 are on the starting line, to the left then to the right. You go forward on the centre line (7 to 12, Kiai on the 12th), then come back toward the start in Kiba Dachi (13 to 18) and with a jump (19, Kiai). Moves 20 to 23 are on the starting line, on the right side: 20 and 23 face the right, 21 faces the left.

Heian Godan 平安五段 kata tutorial

How to read this tutorial

Each move takes one block: on the left, its number and, when there is one, the illustration; on the right, the technique (its Japanese name in bold, the translation below), the stance and the target level. The “Footwork” line explains how to move, and the “Note” line, when there is one, gives a detail to remember.

Jodan is the head (the face); Chudan, the trunk, from the shoulders to the belt (the solar plexus, just under the sternum); Gedan, below the belt (the lower belly). Each level aims at a precise point, not just a height: a chudan punch goes to the solar plexus, not the shoulder. For a block, the level shows the zone being protected.

The shape of the number is the same as on the embusen, to link the two: it shows the stance, and red marks a Kiai.
