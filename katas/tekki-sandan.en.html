Tekki Sandan 鉄騎三段

Iron horseman, third level

Tekki Sandan, the third and last Tekki kata, has 36 moves, with two Kiai: on the 16th and the 36th move. It is the longest of the three. It is required for the black belt grading, 3rd dan. Like the other Tekki, it is done on a single line, sideways.

What this kata trains

The whole kata is done in Kiba Dachi, except for the four crossing steps (Kosa Dachi). Unlike Tekki Shodan and Tekki Nidan, it starts with eight moves in place, a fast series of linked blocks: that is its trademark. It introduces the downward forearm strike (uchi otoshi), the forearm twist (zenwan hineri) and the large circular swing (gedan furisute). It needs a very stable horse stance and blocks that follow each other without a pause.

Embusen 演武線 (floor pattern)

The kata follows a single straight line. Moves 1 to 8 are at the starting point. A crossing step takes you to the right (9 to 17, Kiai on the 16th), another brings you back to the starting point (18 to 25), a third takes you to the left (26 to 32), and a last one brings you back to the starting point (33 to 36, Kiai on the 36th).

Tekki Sandan 鉄騎三段 kata tutorial

How to read this tutorial

Each move takes one block: on the left, its number and, when there is one, the illustration; on the right, the technique (its Japanese name in bold, the translation below), the stance and the target level. The “Footwork” line explains how to move, and the “Note” line, when there is one, gives a detail to remember.

Jodan is the head (the face); Chudan, the trunk, from the shoulders to the belt (the solar plexus, just under the sternum); Gedan, below the belt (the lower belly). Each level aims at a precise point, not just a height: a chudan punch goes to the solar plexus, not the shoulder. For a block, the level shows the zone being protected.

The shape of the number is the same as on the embusen, to link the two: it shows the stance, and red marks a Kiai.
