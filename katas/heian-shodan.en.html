Heian Shodan 平安初段

Peaceful mind, first level

Heian Shodan is the first kata taught at the dojo, required for the grading from white belt to yellow belt. It has 21 moves, with two Kiai: on the 9th and the 17th move.

The name: Heian 平安, peace, tranquillity, and Shodan 初段, first level. Heian Shodan is the “peaceful mind, first level”, the first kata of the Heian series. Here, Shodan gives the kata’s place in the series, not the black belt (see Shodan and the dan ranks).

What this kata trains

Heian Shodan lays the foundations of Shotokan: two stances, three blocks and a stepping attack, on an “I”-shaped embusen. Above all, it teaches switching from hips square (shomen) for the punches to hips side-on (hanmi) for the blocks and the hammer fist.

Embusen 演武線 (line of movement)

The kata moves along an “I”-shaped pattern, also compared to the kanji 工 (kō). The practitioner starts at point 0, facing forward, and returns to that same point at the end of the kata.

Each circle shows the body position (the centre of the stance) after the move. When you pass the same spot again in the other direction, the two moves are drawn side by side: 1 and 3·4, 10 and 12, and on the centre line the way up (6 to 9) next to the way down (14 to 17). The two bars of the “I” are offset: at the bottom, the kata goes further left (2) than right (5); at the top, further right (11) than left (13).

The four shuto uke (moves 18 to 21) are done around the starting point: 18 and 20 are done at the same spot (facing opposite directions), and 19 and 21 step out from there at 45°. With Yame, the practitioner draws back the front foot of the 21st move and returns to Shizentai, at the exact spot where the kata began.

How to read this tutorial

Each move takes one block: on the left, its number and the illustration; on the right, the technique (its Japanese name in bold, the translation below), the stance and the target level. The “Footwork” line explains how to move, and the “Note” line, when there is one, gives a detail to remember.

Jodan is the head (the face); Chudan, the trunk, from the shoulders to the belt (the solar plexus, just under the sternum); Gedan, below the belt (the lower belly). Each level aims at a precise point, not just a height: a chudan punch goes to the solar plexus, not the shoulder. For a block, the level shows the zone being protected.

The shape of the number is the same as on the embusen, to link the two: it shows the stance, and red marks a Kiai.
