Heian Shodan 平安初段

Peaceful mind, first level

Heian Shodan is the first kata taught at the dojo, required for the grading from white belt to yellow belt. It has 21 moves, with two Kiai: on the 9th and the 17th move.

Also read, common to every kata: The spirit of the kata and Common mistakes.

Breaking down the kata 型, step by step

This tutorial assumes a basic understanding of Shotokan terminology and stances. We will use the traditional counting system (Ichi, Ni, San, Shi, Go, Roku, Shichi, Hachi, Ku, Ju, Ju Ichi, Ju Ni, Ju San, Ju Shi, Ju Go, Ju Roku, Ju Shichi, Ju Hachi, Ju Ku, Ni Ju).

Preparation: Yoi 用意 (ready stance)

Musubi Dachi (attention stance). Stand with your heels together, feet turned out at 45 degrees, hands flat along the body, thumbs tucked in.

Heiko Dachi (parallel stance) / Yoi. From Musubi Dachi, open the left foot to the left, then the right foot to the right, so the feet are shoulder-width apart, toes pointing forward. Bring the hands together in front of the lower abdomen, the right fist covered by the left hand, then let them lower naturally along the body into a relaxed ready position.

Embusen 演武線 (line of movement)

The kata moves along an “I”-shaped pattern, also compared to the kanji 工 (kō). The practitioner starts at point 0, facing forward, and returns to that same point at the end of the kata.

Each circle shows the body position (the centre of the stance) after the move. When you pass the same spot again in the other direction, the two moves are drawn side by side: 1 and 3·4, 10 and 12, and on the centre line the way up (6 to 9) next to the way down (14 to 17). The two bars of the “I” are offset: at the bottom, the kata goes further left (2) than right (5); at the top, further right (11) than left (13).

The four shuto uke (moves 18 to 21) are done around the starting point: 18 and 20 are done at the same spot (facing opposite directions), and 19 and 21 step out from there at 45°. With Yame, the practitioner draws back the front foot of the 21st move and returns to Shizentai, at the exact spot where the kata began.

Zenkutsu Dachi 前屈立ち (front stance) Kokutsu Dachi 後屈立ち (back stance) Kiai 気合 Yame 止め (back to the start)
