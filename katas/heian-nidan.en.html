Heian Nidan 平安二段

Peaceful mind, second level

Heian Nidan, the second Heian kata, has 26 moves, with two Kiai: on the 11th and the 26th move. It is required for the grading from yellow belt to orange belt. Its embusen is shaped almost like an “I.”

What this kata trains

Half of the kata is done in Kokutsu Dachi (back stance). The knife-hand block (shuto uke) comes back seven times: it is a central technique of the kata. Heian Nidan is the first kata to teach kicks and two-handed techniques, as well as gyaku hanmi (hips turned the opposite way from the blocking arm). Pay special attention to the side kick and back-fist combination (move 7): you will find it in several other katas.

Embusen 演武線 (line of movement)

The kata follows an “I”-shaped pattern. Moves 1 to 7 are done on the spot, at the starting point, as are 23 and 25. You then go up the centre line (8 to 11, Kiai on the 11th), do the four shuto uke at the far end (12 to 15, two of them on the diagonal), then come back along the same line (16 to 22). Moves 23 to 26 are done at the starting point, with two steps on the diagonal (Kiai on the 26th). Finally you return to the starting point with Yame.

How to read this tutorial

Each move takes one block: on the left, its number and, when there is one, the illustration; on the right, the technique (its Japanese name in bold, the translation below), the stance and the target level. The “Footwork” line explains how to move, and the “Note” line, when there is one, gives a detail to remember.

Jodan is the head (the face); Chudan, the trunk, from the shoulders to the belt (the solar plexus, just under the sternum); Gedan, below the belt (the lower belly). Each level aims at a precise point, not just a height: a chudan punch goes to the solar plexus, not the shoulder. For a block, the level shows the zone being protected.

The shape of the number is the same as on the embusen, to link the two: it shows the stance, and red marks a Kiai.
